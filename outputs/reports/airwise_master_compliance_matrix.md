## Detailed Requirement Analysis & Faculty Defense Notes

### 1. Sensor Calibration (#1) vs Reconstruction (#4) vs Joint TCN (#6)
* **The Academic Trade-off:** Classical Random Forest achieves an overall test calibration $R^2 = 0.8823$, $\text{RMSE} = 30.22\,\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ across all 3,720 test samples ($17.64\,\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ within the Moderate/Poor sub-band, which was previously miscited as the pooled metric). Joint TCN achieves $R^2 = 0.6992$, $\text{RMSE} = 48.30\,\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ under 20% random channel dropout.
* **Defense Justification:** Random Forest is an unconstrained tabular mapping requiring all input features; it completely collapses when a sensor channel fails. Joint TCN optimizes a multi-task Huber loss across dual output heads, using left-padded causal convolutions (receptive field 61h, effective training context 24h). The modest trade-off in calibration accuracy buys operational resilience, enabling real-time streaming imputation and calibration in a unified backbone.
* **Target Distinction in Reconstruction (#4):** Joint TCN evaluated against BAM reference target on the 769 MCAR 20% hidden test points achieved Hidden $\text{RMSE} = 56.76\,\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ (Full Test $\text{RMSE} = 48.30\,\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$). The historical figure of $31.84\,\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ reported in early audit drafts was an evaluation of raw sensor signal recovery, lacks serialized prediction files, is classified as insufficiently documented, and must not be ranked against the BAM-reference reconstruction baselines (Linear 36.93, Bidir GRU 36.17, Causal GRU 44.89, RF 59.03, KNN 68.87).

### 2. Relative Humidity Bias (#2)
* **The Empirical Scope:** Low RH ($<40\%$) RMSE is $35.22\,\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ ($N=254$), Medium RH ($40\text{--}65\%$) RMSE is $42.92\,\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ ($N=411$), and High RH ($>65\%$) RMSE is $15.15\,\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ ($N=81$). Calibration lifts Satisfactory category recall from 41.9% to 96.8%.
* **Confounded Association, Not Causality:** In observational field data across India, relative humidity, site, season, and baseline pollution distributions are heavily confounded. High humidity occurs predominantly during the Bangalore monsoon, where ambient pollution is low ($\text{BAM Mean} \approx 20\,\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$), naturally bounding absolute RMSE. Low humidity coincides with cold, stagnant Delhi winter, where severe pollution spikes exceed $200\text{--}400\,\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$. This result represents an observed statistical association and does not establish humidity as the cause of the observed error difference. We do not claim physical modeling of aerosol hygroscopicity ($\kappa$-Kohler curves).

### 3. Sensor Drift (#3) & Network-Scale Processing (#11)
* **The Honest Limitations:** We explicitly classify both requirements as `NOT ESTABLISHED` and `NOT DEMONSTRATED`.  
* **Defense Integrity:** Decoupling sensor physical aging from inter-annual climatic shifts requires multi-year zero-gas calibration chambers. Demonstrating network-scale processing requires a physical distributed cluster, not single-laptop synthetic duplication. Acknowledging these boundaries strengthens the scientific credibility of the platform.

### 4. Conformal Uncertainty & The 95.70% Metric (#7)
* **The Critical Distinctions:**
  1. Pointwise coverage ($95.70\%$) was obtained with an inflated mean interval width of $200.84\,\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$, which has limited operational utility.
  2. Pointwise coverage must not be conflated with simultaneous 24-hour block coverage ($74.58\%$), which misses the nominal 90% target by $15.42\%$.
  3. Coverage is geographically non-stationary: Bangalore achieves $97.58\%$, while Delhi collapses to $51.07\%$ (and $1.95\%$ during winter spikes $>250\,\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$).
  4. The 24-hour block conformal quantile was derived from 32 calibration blocks; it must not be confused with the 24-hour CPCB daily-mean calibration ($N=78$ windows, width $30.59\,\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$).

### 5. CPCB Regulatory Health Categories (#9) & The 13.43% Difference (#8)
* **The Controlled Reality:** On 127 identical 24-hour test windows, the calibrated model achieves **72.44% accuracy**, outperforming Raw CF=1 (39.37%, $+33.07\%$ gain) and Raw ATM (66.14%, $+6.30\%$ gain).
* **The 13.43% Clarification:** The +13.43 percentage-point difference is the internal accuracy spread between Stable windows (83.33%) and Ambiguous windows (69.90%) within the calibrated model; it is **NOT** the raw-versus-calibrated gain.
* **Trust Signal Boundary:** The category stability index is an operational decision-support heuristic, not a formal Bayesian posterior probability or validated sensor trust score.
* **The Quadratic Kappa Nuance:** Raw ATM achieved a higher Quadratic Weighted Kappa (0.9392 vs 0.9271) because $\kappa_{\text{quad}}$ depends on the complete confusion matrix and marginal distributions; raw ATM had 0 large errors, whereas the calibrated model had 2 large errors despite higher exact accuracy.
* **Scope Qualification:** All analyses evaluate 24-hour PM2.5-based CPCB categories, not multi-pollutant NAQI.

### 6. Big Data Ingestion & Storage Architecture (#10)
* **Infrastructure Scope:** PySpark 4.3.0 ingestion, embedded Derby metastore (`pm25_research.db`), partitioned Parquet (Snappy), and ORC (ZSTD) with read-only DuckDB dashboard querying are fully verified.
* **Storage Reality:** The canonical warehouse tables require $2.126\,\text{MB}$ (CSV), $0.573\,\text{MB}$ (Parquet Snappy, $-73.0\%$), and $0.338\,\text{MB}$ (ORC ZSTD, $-84.1\%$). The earlier 4.8 MB figure represented raw uncompressed source file exports. Standalone HiveServer2, Thrift network daemons, and multi-node HDFS are explicitly not claimed.

### 7. Dataset Provenance & Integrity (#12)
* **Authoritative Citation:** Publicly accessible peer-reviewed dataset from the Dryad Digital Repository (`doi:10.6078/D1RQ70`, version `v20230814`) under Creative Commons Zero v1.0 Universal (**CC0 1.0**).
* **Journal Publication:** Campmier et al. (2023), *Seasonally optimized calibrations improve low-cost sensor performance: long-term field evaluation of PurpleAir sensors in urban and rural India*, **Atmospheric Measurement Techniques**, 16, 4357–4374. DOI: `10.5194/amt-16-4357-2023`.
* **Sampling Dynamics:** Exactly 18,584 predominantly hourly observations across Delhi, Hamirpur, and Bangalore spanning 2018–2021. The designation "predominantly hourly observations" accurately accounts for real-world telemetry dropouts and irregular transmission intervals. Schema validation, row count ($N=18,584$), and null-rate bounds are verified in `dataset_validator.py`. The unverified "100% row checksum" claim has been removed.
